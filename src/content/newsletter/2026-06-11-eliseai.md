> Issue #1 · 2026-06-11 · ~4 min read

**TL;DR**
- EliseAI builds AI "employees" that handle the endless tenant and patient communication that runs operations-heavy businesses — leasing, scheduling, maintenance, intake.
- It just crossed **$200M ARR with a fifth straight year of 100% growth**, and roughly **1 in 6 U.S. apartments** now runs on it.
- The interesting bet isn't housing — it's whether the same engine wins a second hard vertical, healthcare. If it does, EliseAI is a platform, not a point solution.

## What they do
Every operations-heavy business runs on the same boring, expensive work: answering the same questions, scheduling the same appointments, chasing the same overdue payments. EliseAI automates it. In property management, its assistant talks to prospects and residents across text, email, chat, and voice — booking tours, fielding maintenance requests, following up on late rent — the work a leasing office or call center used to do by hand. Since 2022 it's been running the same play in healthcare: patient scheduling, intake, and follow-up for outpatient specialties like dermatology and women's health.

## The market / why now
These are two enormous, communication-choked, labor-starved industries. Property management lives on call centers and leasing staff. Outpatient healthcare is buried in scheduling and intake admin — EliseAI frames it as a roughly $600B mess. The reason this works *now* is simple: LLMs finally hold up across multi-turn, multi-channel conversations well enough to point at real revenue and operations workflows — not just a demo. And in back-office categories, deeply integrated vertical AI tends to beat a horizontal chatbot.

## Traction & growth signals
The numbers are the story here:
- **$200M ARR** as of June 2026 — the **fifth consecutive year of 100% YoY growth**. Doubling every year for five years at this scale is rare air.
- **~1 in 6 U.S. apartments** run on EliseAI (up from ~10% of the market a year earlier), across **600+ owners/operators**, including **75% of NMHC's Top 50**.
- **$250M Series E** led by **a16z** (with Bessemer joining) at a **$2.2B valuation** in August 2025 — double its Series D valuation from a year prior.
- Headcount roughly **doubled to 300+** between the Series D and E, with hundreds more planned for healthcare.

## The thesis — why it could break out
The bull case isn't "AI for landlords." It's that EliseAI has a **repeatable motion**: pick an ops-heavy, communication-bound industry, build the AI workforce for it, integrate deeply enough that ripping it out is painful, then do it again. Housing is the proof. Five years of 100% growth and one-in-six apartments says the model isn't a fluke and the switching costs are real. Healthcare is the test of whether this is a *company* or a *platform*. If the second vertical compounds the way the first did, the $2.2B mark will look early — vertical AI's durable winners are the ones that turn a single wedge into a repeatable land-and-expand engine, and EliseAI is further down that path than almost anyone.

## Risks & open questions
- **Two hard verticals at once.** Healthcare brings HIPAA, clinical-adjacent risk, and a different sales motion. Expansion is the upside *and* the biggest way to lose focus.
- **Margins are a black box.** 100% growth is dazzling, but burn and unit economics aren't public. Growth at any cost reads differently than growth at a profit.
- **The moat question.** As foundation models commoditize conversation, how durable is the edge against property-management incumbents bolting native AI onto their suites?

## What I'd watch next
Healthcare ARR as a share of the total — that's the single number that tells you whether the platform thesis is real. After that: any signal on profitability, and the next raise (or lack of one).
